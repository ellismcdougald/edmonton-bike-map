## Why I Chose This Project

I want to build a project that solves a problem that I have had and that will hopefully be useful to others as well as myself. Finding a good route from one place to another is more challenging on a bicycle than in an automobile. It is not just about finding the fastest or most direct route-- care must be given to the safety and comfort of different roads and paths, as bike users are more vulnerable and exposed to their surroundings than drivers. Current routing options do not always effectively consider these additional factors for bikes. For instance, the suggested route by Apple Maps from my home to the University of Alberta brings me through an area of current construction, has me cross Edmonton's infamously busy and slow University Ave / 114 St intersection, and then directs me down 114 St to access the university (114 St is not a marked bike route and would be quite unpleasant to travel in rush hour traffic). One of the alternative routes recommended by Apple Maps is much more secluded, safe, and overall pleasant ride that avoids traffic (it is a bit hillier than the suggested route, but is also a full kilometre shorter). I want my project to recommend safe and pleasant routes like the latter as opposed to more difficult routes like the one suggested by Apple Maps.

I don't mean to criticize the methodology of Apple and Google Maps' bicycle routing-- I think both platforms do well in making use of the information available to them. They generally attempt to route cyclists towards shared-use paths, bike lanes, and side streets where possible. But the information available is not enough for effective bike routing. For example, a painted bike lane on a busy street will appear to be a good option because it is part of the bike network, but might actually be a rather unpleasant route in practice if traffic is heavy and the bike lane is not adequately protected. A shared use path that switches sides of the road and requires crossing an intersection (which is suprisingly common!) might not be as pleasant to use as a nearby quiet side street.

Not all elements of the bike network are created equal and sometimes roads like a quiet side street which are not part of the bike network may prove to be excellent options for cyclists. I believe that information from cyclists themselves is necessary for effective cyclist routing. That's why Edmonton Bike Map will allow users to give different roads/bike lanes/paths a rating based on their pleasantness/safety/usability and these ratings will then be used by the routing algorithm to recommend safer and more pleasant routes.

## Key Decisions

- I chose Go for the backend because I have heard great things about it and I wnated to give it a try. I had never used Go before this project. Python may have been a better choice because it is good for data processing and also has some useful OpenStreetMaps libraries. But I wanted to try Go and I knew that Go would be more than capable for my needs.
- I chose HTMX for the frontend because I wanted to try it out. I have worked with several different Javascript frameworks in the past: React (and NextJS), Angular, and Vue. The frontend is very simple so HTMX was well-suited.
- Leaflet.js was perfect for my use case-- I wanted to embed an OSM map that I could annotate with routes and information as needed. Leaflet made this very easy to do
- I started with a "minimum prototype": a very basic frontend and backend that could suggest a route and display it on the map. Then, I chose to begin writing tests and implement a continuous integration process. This seemed like a good way to do things: once I had a basic idea and structure established, I implemented CI to ensure that I followed good development practices from this point on.
